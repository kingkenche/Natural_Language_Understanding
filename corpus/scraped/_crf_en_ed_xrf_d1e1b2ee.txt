Energy Dispersive X-Ray Fluorescence Spectrometer (ED-XRF)
Energy Dispersive X-Ray Fluorescence Spectrometer (ED-XRF)
MAKE: Malvern PANalytical
Elemental Analysis technique
Analysis Range: Sodium (Na) to Americium (Am).
Concentration Range: 100 % to ppm level.
X-Ray Source: Ag anode target (15W).
Tube Filters: Six, Software selectable (Blank, Cu 300 µm, Cu 500 µm, Al 50 µm, Al 200 µm, Ti 7 µm, Ag 100 µm)
Detector: Silicon Drift detector with active area 30 mm2, having resolution ≤ 140 eV @ Mn Kα
Ten autosampler positions with spinner, circular sample holders
Software: Omnian (standardless) software for qualitative and quantitative analysis.
Sample Preparation Equipment:
Vibratory cup mill for fine grinding of powders to 50-micron size.
40-ton motorized Hydraulic press with a Die Set of 35 mm.
Quality assurance through elemental analysis of raw materials like limestone, clays, and final cement products.
Rapidly determine the elemental composition in ores, rocks, and minerals for exploration and quality control.
Analysis of heavy metals and pollutants in soil, sediments, and airborne particulates.
Sample Form: Solid, liquid, Powder, Paste.
Dust-free flat solid sample.
Solid block/pallets of circular shape having a flat diameter from 28 to 50 mm.
Samples in powder form should be of a grain size around 50 microns, and preferred to make pellets for a good signal-to-noise ratio.
The minimum amount of sample needed to make a pellet is 10 grams. Boric Acid is used as a binding agent for making pellets.
Liquid samples can also be analyzed.
For quantification, loss on ignition (LOI) and free moisture parameters are needed and provided by the user.
Quantification is performed using the Omnian method, a standardless method.
Quantification can be provided in oxide form and elemental form, depending on the user's requirement.
