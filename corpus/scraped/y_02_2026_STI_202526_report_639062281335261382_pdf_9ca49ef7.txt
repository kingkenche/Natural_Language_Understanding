2025-26stih@cetsd.iitj.ac.inSTI HUB ANNUAL REPORTINTRODUCTIONThe Science Technology and Innovation (STI) Hub of IIT Jodhpur hasstarted creating impactful changes in the overall quality of life of theScheduled Caste (SC) communities located in the Thar Desert region ofIndia The project has been running in 5 districts of North- WesternRajasthan namely Barmer, Bikaner, Jaisalmer, Jodhpur, and Nagaur. STI focuses on empowering the most vulnerable and marginalizedsections of the society. Initiatives include creating and maintainingsustainable livelihood opportunities, ensuring nutritional intake throughhomesteading by low-cost ceramic irrigation systems, improving theavailability of clean drinking water using g- filters, and propermanagement of animal dung. The STI hub project can be accessed onthe web through the CETSD website https://cetsd.iitj.ac.in/?page_id=1319. ROPE ANDALLIEDPRODUCTS 2025-26Rope and Allied ProductsSTI HUB ANNUAL REPORTROPE AND ALLIED PRODUCTSThe STI Project sought to integrate simple yet effective technologicalinterventions into the traditional craft of rope-making and the productionof associated artifacts. The introduction of mechanized equipment,including both manual and electronic ratt machines, significantly reducedthe drudgery involved in the process. These machines operate efficientlywith both local natural fibers and recycled cloth fibers, enabling thecreation of a diverse range of value-added products. The combination ofthese technologies with new design innovations—targeted toward local andregional market demands—has encouraged greater participation andadoption of the initiative among the beneficiaries.Beneficiaries are now actively engaged in managing natural fiber resourcesand producing value-added products from locally available materials. Theproject thus fosters the conservation and revitalization of indigenousknowledge systems while creating sustainable livelihood opportunities.Moreover, it directly contributes to the achievement of SustainableDevelopment Goal (SDG) 8 on decent work and economic growth, andindirectly advances SDGs 1, 2, and 3, which focus on poverty reduction,zero hunger, and good health and well-being. In addition, the workundertaken through the STI Hub aligns with the objectives of nationalinitiatives such as the Pradhan Mantri Anusuchit Jaati Abhuyday Yojana(PM-AJAY), which aims to promote poverty alleviation and the socialempowerment of Scheduled Caste communities.Satellite CentersThat-JaisalmerSajiyali-BarmerPipar-JodhpurPachala Sidha -NagaurKakoo Bikaner

Cultivation with SSPV atNandiya Khurd SSPV Beneficiary atNandiya JajraCultivation of Lehsun andOnion with SSPV 2025-26Sub Surface Porous VesselSTI HUB ANNUAL REPORTSUB SURFACE POROUS VESSELSSSPVs and G-filters are produced using the same fabrication equipment, with the primary distinctionlying in the proportion of raw materials used. The Sub-Surface Porous Vessel (SSPV) technologyinvolves a frustum-shaped ceramic vessel designed as an irrigation system particularly suited for aridregions. This system ensures high water-use efficiency, maintaining soil moisture for extended periods.Moreover, as water delivery is confined to the root zone, evaporation losses are eliminated. Anadditional advantage of the SSPV system is that it operates without any requirement for externalenergy input.The adoption of SSPV technology in irrigation aligns with the objectives of water-use efficiency andnutrient management outlined under the National Mission for Sustainable Agriculture (NMSA). In aridareas such as North-Western Rajasthan, its use directly enhances the nutritional intake of ruralcommunities through homesteading and small-scale cultivation. Furthermore, given its irrigationefficiency and negligible evaporation loss, the technology holds significant potential for promotingafforestation in desert environments. At STI, comprehensive training has been provided across allclusters for the production and use of both SSPVs and G-filters.Satellite CentersThat-JaisalmerSajiyali-BarmerPipar-JodhpurPachala Sidha -NagaurKakoo Bikaner
