Offered for  M.Tech and PhD
The Instructor will:
1. Enable a student to face the challenges in communication, primarily in a technical milieu.
2.Enhance  a  student’s  English  Language  skills  namely  listening,  speaking,  reading  and  writing
(LSRW) and the role played by them in the process of communication.
3.  Make  students  conversant  in  addressing  communication  challenges  that impact  organizations
through sharing of best practices and group learning.
The students are expected to have the ability to:
1.  Comprehend,  recognize  and  articulate  the  formal  elements  of  specific  genres  of  technical
2.To  gain  a  familiarity  with  practical  and  theoretical  aspects  of  various  trends  and  debates  in
3. To develop a sensibility and appreciation for some of the ideas, issues, and problems involved
in writing about technology and in workplace writing.
Contents Part I (HSS Faculty) Total –  07 hours (07 Lectures)
Introduction to Communication – Introduction to Communication and the Importance of
Communicating in English, Role of Emotions in Communication, Basics of Communication,
Purpose and Audience, Cross-Cultural Communication, Language as a tool of communication,
LSRW. (1 hour)
Listening -Introduction to Listening, Types of Listening, Active Listening, Barriers to Listening,
Listening to pre-recorded audio visual material/film. (1 hour)
Speaking –Introduction to Speaking, Mock Personal Interviews, Body Language, Organizing and
learning to make a presentation, Phonetics and Basic Pronunciation, Common Errors in English,
Telephone Etiquette, Group Discussions.(2 hour)
Reading - Purpose of Reading, Topic Sentence and its role, Reading for different purposes,
Reading Comprehension, Reading Techniques. (1 hour)
Writing– Introduction, Importance, Characteristics, Impersonal and Formal language, Elements
of Style, Techniques for Good Technical Writing, Paragraph Writing, Writing a Formal Letter,
Writing an Email, Plagiarism. (2 hours)
Part II (Faculty of Engineering and Sciences) – 07 hours (07 Lectures)
Introduction to  Technical Communication
Writing – Mechanics of Technical Writing, Organization and Structure in Technical Writing,
Writing an abstract, Writing a Research Paper, Writing a Review, Report Writing, Writing
Technical descriptions, Copyediting and formatting, Referencing, Citations, Plagiarism.(4 hours)
Reading – Identifying Language Errors, Reading Comprehension with specific reference to
passages/articles in Science and Technology. (1 hour)
Lectures with Hands on Training – Grammarly, End note, Language software, Document
processing using software.(2 hours)

Department  Humanities and Social Sciences
L-T-P [C]  3-0-0
Offered for  B. Tech., M. Tech. and Ph.D. Programs
1.To  help  students  explore  the  intricacies  of  cultural  forms  and  practices  in  the
larger context of the political lives of the Indian nation
2.To help students become familiar with some of the most significant events in the
history of post-independence India through the prism of film history and state policy
3.To make students familiar with conceptual apparatuses in cinema studies and with
the overlaps between political and cultural histories
The students are expected to have the ability to:
1.Read archival and other materials that are relevant for history-writing
2.Explore associations between public institutions, national history, mass and avant-
garde culture and the ever-changing socio-politico-cultural dynamics
3.Develop  acquaintance  with  contemporary  scholarly  writings  on  Indian  history,
cinema and politics

relevance in Indian politics, literature review (6 lectures)

negotiations,  carrying  out  cine-archival  research,  reading  of  film  industry  and  film
society  materials/publications,  review  of  relevant  theoretical  schools  in  Indian
cinema studies (12 lectures)

Prime Minister’s 20-point program, role of I&B ministry, industrial responses, Indian
New  Wave  and  state-sponsored  cinema  in  the  1970s,  debates  on  sponsorship  and
film economy (12 lectures)
Globalization  of  the  Dictatorial  in  the  1970s:  Digital  Remembering–  The  idea  of  the
